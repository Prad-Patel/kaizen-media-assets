Most people use Claude as a clever text box. You type, it answers, and nothing it says connects to the rest of your working day. Connectors change that. Once you link Claude to your Gmail, Google Calendar and Google Drive, it can look at your real emails, diary and documents instead of guessing, and it can draft the work for you to check.

This is a beginner's guide. It takes about ten minutes to connect all three, and then we give you one job to try with each, with the exact words to paste. You need a Claude account and a Google account. Nothing else.

What connecting actually means

A connector is a permission you give Claude to read from, and in some cases write to, another service. According to Anthropic's help centre, the Gmail, Google Calendar and Google Drive connectors are available to all users of Claude and Claude Desktop. If you are on a Team or Enterprise plan, an owner has to switch connectors on for the organisation first. Claude can only see the data in the Google account you connect.

The help centre also says what each one can do. Gmail: search and read emails, list drafts, and write drafts, replies and forwards. Sending, replying and forwarding need your approval by default. Calendar: view events, find times when people are free, and create or change events. Drive: search and read your Docs, Sheets, Slides and PDFs, and save files Claude creates. Sharing, moving and trashing files need your approval by default.

Step 1: Connect your Google account

Open Claude and go to your settings. Anthropic's connectors getting started guide says to look for Connectors there, click Connect next to the service you want, sign in to Google and approve the permissions. Menu names move around from time to time, so if yours says Customize rather than Settings, look for the same word, Connectors.

Connect Gmail, Calendar and Drive in turn. Once they are connected, you can switch each one on or off for a particular chat by clicking the plus sign below the message box and choosing Connectors.

If you use a Google Workspace account at work and see an "Access blocked" message, your Workspace administrator may need to mark Claude as a trusted app. The help centre gives the admin steps, and they usually take effect after about 15 minutes.

Job 1: Gmail, a morning inbox sort

Start with something you do every day anyway. Paste this into a new chat with Gmail switched on:

Look at my emails from the last two days. List any that are from customers or suppliers and need a reply from me, most urgent first, with one line on what each one wants. Then write a short, friendly draft reply to the top three and save them as drafts. Do not send anything.

What you get is a short list and three drafts waiting in your Gmail drafts folder. You read them, change what you want, and press send yourself. The last sentence of the prompt matters: it keeps you in charge.

Job 2: Calendar, find the time and book it

Booking a site visit, a call or a delivery usually means flicking between your diary and your inbox. Try this with Calendar switched on:

Look at my calendar for next week. Find three one-hour slots that leave at least 30 minutes either side of other appointments and are not before 9am or after 5pm. List them, and tell me which day looks busiest.

Then reply with the slot you want and ask Claude to add the appointment, with the customer's name in the title. Claude can create and change events, so check the entry in your calendar afterwards the first few times you do this.

Job 3: Drive, build a quote from what you already have

Most small businesses have a price list somewhere in Drive and a folder of old quotes. Claude can read both. With Drive switched on, try:

Find my price list and the three most recent quotes in Google Drive. Using the same layout and wording as those quotes, write a new quote for [customer name] for [the job]. Show me which prices came from which document, and flag anything you had to guess.

The last line is the useful one. Asking Claude to show its sources and flag its guesses means you can check a quote in a minute rather than redoing it. Never send a figure to a customer that you have not checked against your own price list.

What it could give back

Here is an illustration, not a promise. Suppose you spend ten minutes a day hunting through your inbox, diary and folders for the email, the free slot or the latest price list. Ten minutes a day, five days a week, for 48 weeks is 2,400 minutes, which is 40 hours a year. If a connected Claude halves that, you have won back about 20 hours. Time your own week for a few days first, because your figure will be different from ours.

The bigger gain is often less tidy to measure: fewer replies left until tomorrow, fewer double-booked days, quotes that go out while the customer is still interested.

One thing to watch

A connector gives Claude access to a real account, so start with drafts and lists rather than anything that sends or deletes. Anthropic's defaults already ask for your approval before Claude sends an email or shares a file, so leave those on. We have not run these exact prompts on a client's account, and the steps above come from Anthropic's own help pages as they stood on 28 October 2026. Check what your own plan shows, because features and menu names change.
