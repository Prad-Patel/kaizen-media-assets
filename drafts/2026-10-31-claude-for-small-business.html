In May, Anthropic launched Claude for Small Business: fifteen ready-made workflows for jobs like chasing invoices, closing the month and sorting new leads. It is aimed at US owners first, and nobody has told us yet when UK firms get the full package. But the idea behind it is not locked to one country, and you can borrow most of it this afternoon. This is what is in it, what we do not know, and how to copy the best bits.

What Claude for Small Business actually is

According to Anthropic's announcement, published on 13 May 2026, it is a bundle of 15 ready-to-run workflows plus 15 separate skills, covering finance, operations, sales, marketing, HR and customer service. It runs as a switch inside Claude Cowork, Anthropic's desktop app for getting work done with Claude.

The workflows Anthropic names include planning payroll, closing the month, getting a pulse on your business, running your next campaign, an invoice chaser, a margin analyser, a month-end prepper, a tax-season organiser, a contract reviewer, a lead triager and a content strategist. Four more are on Anthropic's solutions page rather than in the announcement.

It connects to the tools a small firm already pays for: PayPal, QuickBooks, HubSpot, Canva, Docusign, Google Workspace and Microsoft 365. The rule that matters most is this one, in Anthropic's own words: "Claude does the work; you approve before anything sends, posts, or pays."

What Anthropic has not told us

We read the announcement in full, and three things are missing. It does not say which Claude plan you need. It does not give a price. And it does not mention the UK or any market outside the US, where every stop on Anthropic's launch tour is. So if you are in Britain, check your own Claude account to see what you can switch on before you plan around it. We have not used Claude for Small Business ourselves, and we are not going to pretend we have.

The announcement also gives no time-saving figure. The customer quotes carry no numbers either, so we will not quote one for you. Where we give a figure below, it is our own arithmetic, and we show the working.

Why this matters in the UK anyway

Simply Business surveyed 1,842 UK small businesses for its 2026 report. Almost half (47%) now use AI tools, up from 22% the year before. But only 19% describe themselves as very confident using it day to day, and 39% say the main barrier is not seeing a clear use for it.

That second number is the useful one. Claude for Small Business is, in effect, Anthropic answering the question "what would I actually use it for?" with a list of fifteen jobs. You do not need the product to benefit from the list.

Three jobs from the list you can copy today

You can do a version of each of these in a normal Claude chat with a few pasted lines. These prompts are our adaptations of the job names, not Anthropic's wording.

1. The invoice chaser

Paste a list of overdue invoices (client, amount, days late) and ask:

"Here are my overdue invoices. Put them in order of who to chase first. For each, draft a short, polite reminder in a friendly British tone. For anything over 30 days late, make it firmer. Do not send anything."

You read the drafts and send them yourself. With the connected version, the point is that Claude would queue the reminders for your approval instead of you pasting anything.

2. The margin analyser

Paste a simple table of what you sold, what each item cost you and what you charged:

"Work out the profit margin on each line as a percentage. Show me the three lowest and the three highest. Suggest one change to improve each of the three lowest, and tell me what you are assuming."

Check the sums against your own figures before you act on any of it. Claude can slip on arithmetic, so treat its answer as a first pass.

3. The lead triager

Paste the last ten enquiries you received:

"Sort these enquiries into hot, warm and cold, with one line on why. For the hot ones, draft a reply that offers two times for a call this week."

Tell it what a good lead looks like for you first (job size, location, budget), or it will guess.

What it could be worth

Here is the arithmetic, on our assumptions and not Anthropic's. Say the money admin in your week (chasing payments, checking margins, sorting enquiries) takes three hours. If a ready-made workflow trims a third of that, you get an hour back a week. Over 48 working weeks that is about 48 hours, or six working days. If your time is worth £30 an hour, that is roughly £1,440 a year of time. Your numbers will differ, so time your own week first.

That is an illustration, not a promise. The tools cost money too, and the saving only exists if you actually use the drafts.

One thing to watch

Connected workflows can touch real money and real customers. Keep the approval step switched on, read every draft, and start with a single job before you connect more.

Where to start

Pick one job from the fifteen that eats your week, try the copy-paste version above, and time it against doing it by hand for a fortnight. If it earns its place, then look at connecting your accounting or CRM tool so Claude can read the real figures.
