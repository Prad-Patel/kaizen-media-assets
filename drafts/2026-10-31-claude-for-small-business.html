Anthropic's Claude for Small Business has grown quickly since its May launch. Its September update says it now has 43 workflows, and its own pages list 37 tools it connects to. That is a lot to take in, so here is everything Anthropic has actually named, grouped by the job it does, with the three we would try first. We have not used it ourselves, and there is no UK date or UK pricing on any of Anthropic's pages, so treat this as a map of what exists, not a promise of what you can switch on in Britain today.

What it is and what it costs

It is a plugin that runs inside Claude Cowork, Anthropic's desktop app. According to Anthropic's September update, it is available on every paid Claude plan. Anthropic's small business page describes Pro for one person and Team for a business with more than one, both month to month, but lists no prices. Setup is meant to need no IT help, and most workflows still run if you have no connector, using an uploaded spreadsheet or a forwarded email.

The rule that matters most is approval. Workflows start in approval mode, so Claude drafts and prepares and you decide. Payroll is staged for you to submit, bills wait for your approval before payment, and nothing sends without your say-so by default.

Everything Anthropic has named, by job

Anthropic says there are 43 workflows but names only some of them publicly. This is every one we could find on its pages on 31 October 2026.

Money and the books

  • Close the month (/close-month): reconciles your books against payment processors, flags what does not match, writes a plain-English profit and loss and a close packet for your accountant.
  • Plan payroll (/plan-payroll): checks you will have the cash, chases overdue invoices and readies the payroll run for you to submit.
  • Pay the bills (/pay-the-bills): handles bills, with payment waiting for your approval.
  • Tax prep (/tax-prep): handles quarterly taxes or 1099s. Those are US terms, so check what applies to a UK firm before relying on it.
  • Invoice chaser, margin analyser, month-end prepper and tax-season organiser: named in the May launch alongside the workflows above.

Running the week

  • Monday brief (/monday-brief): cash, sales, pipeline, your calendar and your top priority on one page. Anthropic's example posts it to Slack at 6:30am.
  • Restock (/restock): handles reorders.
  • Scheduled automations: workflows can run every Monday, at month end, or when a new lead arrives.

Winning and keeping customers

  • Speed to lead (/speed-to-lead): answers new enquiries on nights and weekends with real meeting times and logs them in your CRM. Nothing sends until you approve.
  • Grow pipeline (/grow-pipeline): finds prospects who resemble your best customers and writes outreach in your voice.
  • Call list (/call-list): tells you who to call today.
  • Proposal builder (/proposal-builder): turns a voice memo and site photos into a proposal in your format, priced from your last three deals.
  • Win back lapsed customers: targets people who have stopped buying.
  • Lead triager and contract reviewer: named in the May launch.

Marketing

  • Marketing Monday (/marketing-monday): what is working, what customers are saying and what competitors changed.
  • Social content engine (/social-content-engine): plans next week's content, for example a two-week posting calendar with captions for your approval.
  • Run your next campaign and content strategist: find the slow stretch in your revenue, draft the promotion and generate the assets in Canva.

Setting it up

  • Onboarding (/smb-onboard): learns your business and helps you connect your tools.
  • Build connector (/build-connector): links a tool with no native connector, through Zapier.
  • Build agent (/build-agent): turns a recurring manual task into a workflow you can run by name or on a schedule.

Anthropic names no separate HR or customer service workflows. Payroll sits under money, and speed to lead is the nearest thing to customer service.

Three jobs to try today

You can do a rough version of the first three in a normal Claude chat with a few pasted lines. These prompts are our adaptations of the job names, not Anthropic's wording, and you read every draft before anything goes out.

Chase invoices. Paste a list of overdue invoices and ask: "Put these in order of who to chase first. For each, draft a short, polite reminder in a friendly British tone, firmer for anything over 30 days late. Do not send anything."

Check margins. Paste what you sold, what each item cost and what you charged: "Work out the profit margin on each line as a percentage. Show me the three lowest and suggest one change for each, and tell me what you are assuming." Check the sums yourself before you act on them.

Sort leads. Paste your last ten enquiries: "Sort these into hot, warm and cold with one line on why. For the hot ones, draft a reply offering two times for a call this week."

What it could be worth

Here is arithmetic on our assumptions, not Anthropic's. Say the money admin in your week (chasing payments, checking margins, sorting enquiries) takes three hours. If a workflow trims a third of that, you get an hour back a week. Over 48 working weeks that is about 48 hours, or six working days. At £30 an hour that is roughly £1,440 a year of time. Your numbers will differ, so time your own week first. It is an illustration, the plans cost money, and the saving only exists if you use the drafts.

Where to start

Do not switch on forty workflows. Pick the one job that eats your week, try the copy-paste version for a fortnight, and compare it with doing it by hand. If it earns its place, connect your accounting or CRM tool so Claude can read the real figures.
