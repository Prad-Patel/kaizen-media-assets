Claude Duet is the first Claude that messages you before you message it, and it is not available in the UK yet. Anthropic launched it on 21 October as a research preview, and the reports we have seen say it is limited to US Pro and Max accounts through a waitlist, with no UK date announced. So this is not a "go and use it today" post. It is the post for the owner who would rather be ready on day one than start from a blank screen, and everything below you can do this week with the Claude you already have.

What Claude Duet is, and where it stands

Per 9to5Google's launch report and TechCrunch's coverage, Duet is a proactive agent. Instead of waiting for a prompt, it keeps an eye on the apps you connect and comes to you with what needs attention. Coverage describes jobs such as a morning summary, drafted email replies, meeting preparation and chasing unpaid invoices. It asks for your approval before it sends a message, makes a payment, signs a document or deletes anything.

Two honest caveats. First, availability: it is an invite-only preview, and the access details come mostly from secondary write-ups rather than Anthropic's own pages, so check Anthropic directly before relying on them. Second, we have not used Duet. Nothing here describes our experience of it, only what has been reported and what we would set up in advance.

Why setting up now is worth 20 minutes

Duet reportedly uses the memory, skills and connectors you have already set up in Claude. That means the quality of what it does on day one depends on groundwork you can do today. A proactive assistant with nothing connected and no instructions has nothing useful to say.

Here is the potential prize, worked through on our own illustrative numbers rather than a sourced result. If checking email, calendar and unpaid invoices each morning takes you 20 minutes, and an assistant that arrives with a ready summary halves it, you get 10 minutes back. Ten minutes, five days a week, 48 weeks a year, is 40 hours. Your numbers will differ, but it shows why the morning triage is the job to aim at first.

The 20-minute setup

Step 1: connect the three apps that hold your day

In Claude, open the connectors settings and connect your email, your calendar and your cloud drive (Gmail, Google Calendar and Google Drive if you use Google Workspace). Start read-only where the connector gives you the choice. Which plan you need for connectors changes, so check what your own account offers.

Step 2: write a one-page "how my business works" note

Open a Claude Project and add a short note: what you sell, who your best customers are, your usual turnaround, what counts as urgent, and the three things you never want sent without your say-so. Our guide to setting up a Claude Project covers the click path.

Step 3: run a manual morning brief

Paste this into your Project each morning for a week:

Look at my email, calendar and files from the last 24 hours. Give me: (1) anything that needs a reply today, with a one-line draft; (2) any meeting I should prepare for and what to read first; (3) anything waiting on a payment or a decision. Do not send or change anything. Keep it under 200 words.

This is the manual version of what Duet is built to do on its own. Each morning, note what it got wrong. Those notes become your rules later.

Step 4: write your approval rules

Duet reportedly asks before it sends, pays, signs or deletes. Decide your own line now. Our suggestion: it may draft anything, it may never send to a customer without you reading it, and it never touches money. Put those three lines in your Project note so they are ready when a proactive tool arrives.

The catch to plan for: usage

Reports of the preview say it shares your existing Claude limits, and that connectors are token-intensive. Background checking could use your allowance faster than you expect. The exact share a check uses has not been confirmed in the sources we found, so the manual week above is also a cheap way to see how much a daily brief uses before anything runs on its own.

What to watch for in your first week

Use the manual brief as a test, not a replacement for your own judgement. Anthropic itself warns that the early version of Duet may misread correspondence or misjudge how urgent something is, and the same is true of any assistant reading your inbox. In week one, check three things each morning. Did it surface anything urgent that you would have missed? Did it flag anything as urgent that was not? And did the one-line draft replies sound like you? If the answer to the third is no, add two or three examples of your own emails to the Project note and try again.

By Friday you will have a written record of what works, which connectors earned their place and which jobs are worth handing over. That is the real groundwork, and it is the same whether the assistant is Duet, a competitor or a routine you run yourself.

What we would do

If a client asked us whether to wait for Duet, we would tell them to spend the 20 minutes now. If Duet reaches the UK, you are ready. If it takes months, you still have a morning brief and a set of written rules that save time today.
