What has actually launched

Cloudways AI Website Builder. Cloudways, owned by DigitalOcean, announced the full launch on 16 November 2026. You type a description of your business and it builds a WordPress site, or a WooCommerce shop with product pages and a basket. During its public preview, Cloudways says sites took about four minutes on average to build. The pages are made from standard WordPress blocks rather than a locked, proprietary format, so you can keep editing them in WordPress like any other site. If you sell online, you can update listings and create discount codes by typing what you want in plain English.

Bluehost AI Builder. Bluehost runs a conversational builder: you describe the business, answer a few questions, and it produces a WordPress site you can then refine by asking for changes. Its older WordPress tool, WonderSuite, has been renamed WordPress AI Builder and comes included with Bluehost's WordPress hosting. The newer AI Builder is the one most owners will want, because the UK pricing page lists a free tier with no card needed.

How to do it this weekend

  1. Write down four things first: what you sell, who buys it, where you work, and the one thing you want a visitor to do (call, book, buy or ask for a quote).
  2. Pick one builder. If you have never run a website, start with Bluehost's free tier: nothing to pay until you want your own domain. If you already host with Cloudways, or you want a shop, use Cloudways.
  3. Paste the simple prompt below, with your details filled in.
  4. Swap the stock photos for your own. Real photos of your work, your team and your premises do more for trust than anything the AI can pick.
  5. Read every line before you publish. Check prices, opening hours, your phone number and any claim the AI has made on your behalf.
  6. Connect your domain and go live.

The simple prompt

Build a website for [business name], a [what you do] in [town]. Pages: Home, Services, About, Reviews and Contact. The main goal is to get visitors to [call us / book online / ask for a quote], so put that button at the top of every page.

That is enough to get a first draft you can react to. If you want a draft that needs far less fixing, use the detailed version.

The detailed prompt

Role and goal: You are a web designer who builds clear, fast websites for UK small businesses. Build a five-page website whose only job is to turn visitors into [enquiries / bookings / sales].

About my business (fill in):
Business name: [ ]
What we do, in one sentence: [ ]
Where we work (towns or postcodes covered): [ ]
Our three main services or products, with a starting price if we publish one: [ ]
Who our best customers are: [ ]
Why customers choose us over others: [ ]
Opening hours, phone number and email: [ ]
Two or three real customer reviews, word for word: [ ]

Hard rules: Use only the facts above; do not invent reviews, awards, years in business, prices or guarantees. Use British spelling. Put our phone number and main call to action at the top of every page. Keep every page readable on a phone.

Preferences: A warm, plain-English tone, short paragraphs, and colours that suit [describe your brand or paste your logo colours].

Output layout: Home (headline, one-line promise, three services, one review, call to action); Services (one section per service); About (who we are and where we work); Reviews; Contact (form, phone, email, hours, area covered).

Before you start: Ask me up to five questions about anything that is missing or unclear. Do not guess.

Why it is built this way: the AI fills gaps with plausible-sounding invention, so the labelled blocks give it your real facts and the hard rules forbid it from making up the rest. Keeping preferences separate from rules tells it what it can bend. The fixed layout stops it wandering into pages you do not need, and the questions step catches the gaps before they turn into confident nonsense on your homepage.

Where a person still earns their fee

An AI builder gets you a clean, working brochure site. It does not do the thinking behind it. Three places we would still bring in help:

  • Anything custom. Booking systems tied to your calendar, quote calculators, customer portals or links into your accounts software are still build work.
  • Being found on Google. A new site starts with no reputation. Local search, your Google Business Profile and genuinely useful pages are what bring visitors, and none of that comes from one sentence.
  • Your edit allowance. Both builders cap AI changes (Cloudways includes 20 a month per site). Plan your changes in batches rather than tweaking one word at a time.

One thing to watch: these builders run on the provider's hosting, so moving away later takes some effort. Because both produce standard WordPress, it is very doable, but ask how to export your site before you commit.
