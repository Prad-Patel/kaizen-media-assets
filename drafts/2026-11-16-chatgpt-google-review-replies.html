85% of UK shoppers check at least one review site before they buy, according to a Feefo and Censuswide survey of 3,000 UK consumers this February. Your Google reviews are being read, and so are your replies, or the silence where a reply should be. The good news: ChatGPT can draft a week's worth of review replies in one go, in your own voice, and the whole job fits in about 10 minutes a week. Here is exactly how we would set it up for a client, with the prompts to copy.

Why replying to reviews is worth 10 minutes a week

People do not just read the stars. They read how you handled the three-star one. In BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey, 89% of consumers said they expect business owners to respond to reviews. That survey was of 1,002 US adults, so treat it as a strong signal rather than a UK figure, but the UK number above tells the same story: reviews are part of the buying decision.

Replies matter for two more reasons. Google tells the reviewer when you reply, so a thank-you lands with the customer directly, and a calm, helpful reply to a bad review is often more persuasive to the next reader than ten five-star ones. Yet replying is the job that slips, because every reply feels like it needs a fresh bit of thinking. That is the part ChatGPT is good at.

Google is testing its own "reply with AI" button inside Business Profile, but Search Engine Land reports the test is limited to the US, Brazil and India. For UK businesses today, a ChatGPT chat is the quickest route, and the free plan is enough because you are only pasting in text.

Step 1: Write your house style once

Generic replies are the problem, not the solution. "Thanks for your feedback, we value all our customers" reads like nobody looked. So before anything else, spend five minutes telling ChatGPT how you sound. Copy this, fill in the brackets and save it somewhere you can find it again, such as a note on your phone:

You write Google review replies for [business name], a [what you do] in [town]. Our tone is [friendly and plain / warm and professional]. Keep every reply under 60 words. Thank the reviewer by first name, mention one specific thing they said, and sign off as [your name] at [business name]. Never use the phrases "we value your feedback" or "sorry for any inconvenience". Never mention prices, refunds or personal details in public.

If your ChatGPT plan has Projects, you can paste this into a project's instructions so it is applied every time. If not, paste it at the top of each new chat. Either works.

Step 2: Collect the week's reviews in one go

Pick one slot a week, say Monday morning with your first coffee. Open your Business Profile (search your business name on Google while signed in, or use the Google Business Profile app) and select Read reviews. Copy any new reviews you have not answered yet, including the reviewer's first name and star rating. Five reviews takes about two minutes to gather.

Reviews are already public, so pasting them into ChatGPT is fine. Do not add anything private, such as the customer's phone number, address or job details from your records.

Step 3: Paste the batch prompt

Now paste your house style, then this:

Here are this week's Google reviews. Write one reply for each, numbered to match. Follow the house style above. For 4 and 5 star reviews, keep it short and warm. For anything 3 stars or below, use the bad review rules I will give you next. If anything is unclear, ask me before you write. [paste reviews, one per line, with first name and stars]

You will get a numbered list of drafts in a few seconds. Read each one. You will usually want to change a word or two, and that is the point: you are editing, not writing from a blank box.

Step 4: Handle the bad review properly

A one-star review is where a rushed reply does real damage. ChatGPT helps most here, because it stays calm when you might not. Paste this when a review stings:

This is a negative review. Draft a reply that thanks them for raising it, owns anything we got wrong without arguing, does not repeat private details, and invites them to call [number] or email [address] so we can put it right. No excuses, no defensiveness. Under 70 words.

Then step away for ten minutes before you post it. If the review breaks Google's rules (spam, abuse, or someone who was never your customer), you can flag it for removal instead, but reply calmly either way, because other customers are reading.

Step 5: Post the replies

Back in Business Profile, select Reply next to each review, paste the edited draft and select Reply again. Google's own help page notes three things worth knowing: your profile has to be verified before you can reply, Google checks replies against its content policies before they appear, and the reviewer is notified when you reply. You can also edit or delete a reply later.

What it could give back

Here is an illustration, on these assumptions: a business gets five new reviews a week and writes each reply from scratch in about four minutes. That is 20 minutes a week, and in practice it often does not happen at all. With the house style saved and one batch prompt, the same five replies take about 10 minutes including reading and posting. That is roughly eight hours a year back on 48 weeks, and, more importantly, every review gets an answer.

The bigger prize is not the time. It is the next customer who reads your page and sees a business that answers, thanks people by name and handles complaints like an adult. With 85% of UK shoppers checking reviews before they buy, that page is doing your selling while you are on a job.

One thing to watch

Read every reply before it goes out. ChatGPT can misread sarcasm, mix up two customers or promise something you would not. You are the one posting it, so it should sound like you on a good day. If a draft does not, tell ChatGPT what is wrong ("too formal", "do not offer a discount") and ask again.

Start this week

  • Write your house style message today and save it.
  • Pick a weekly slot and put it in your calendar.
  • Reply to your last five unanswered reviews, oldest first.
