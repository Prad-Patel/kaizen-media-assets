47% of UK small business owners now use AI tools. A year ago the figure was 22%. That is the headline from Simply Business's 2026 SME Insights Report, which surveyed 1,842 UK small businesses. In other words, the number has more than doubled in twelve months, and a further 13% say they plan to start within the next six to 12 months. We have seen the figures through the insurer's own news page and trade coverage rather than the full report, so treat the exact percentages as the survey's, not ours.

The uncomfortable part sits underneath. Only 19% of owners describe themselves as "very confident" using AI day to day, and 33% say they use it only for routine admin. Plenty of firms have signed up. Far fewer have found a job where it earns its keep. This post is about picking those first jobs.

What the survey does and does not say

Different surveys give very different answers on how many firms use AI, because they define "use" differently. Simply Business counts owners who use AI tools at all, which gives 47%. A separate study from Enterprise Nation found that 21% of small businesses use AI regularly and only 6% have embedded it into daily work across the business. Both can be true at once. Trying a chatbot once a month is not the same as having it handle your quote follow-ups every Monday.

You can read the headline numbers on Simply Business's own write-up, and a summary of the adoption jump on BM Magazine. The Simply Business figure is for small business owners, while some other reports count a broader group of SMEs, so do not mix the numbers up when you quote them.

Why the gap between "use" and "use well" matters

If half of your competitors are dabbling, the advantage does not go to whoever signs up. It goes to whoever picks two or three repeatable jobs and does them every week. Simply Business puts the gap down mainly to a lack of clarity about practical applications, not to access. That matches what we hear: owners are not short of tools, they are short of a first job that is small enough to try on a Tuesday afternoon.

Five jobs to start with this week

Each of these takes under half an hour to set up, works with a free or low-cost AI assistant, and is something you do already.

1. Turn your enquiry replies into templates

Take the last five enquiries you answered. Paste them in, remove names and phone numbers, and ask the assistant to write three reusable replies in your own voice: one for a quote, one for availability and one for "sorry, outside our area". Save them and edit each one before sending.

2. Draft the follow-up you keep putting off

Quotes that go quiet are money sitting in your sent folder. Ask the assistant to draft a polite two-line nudge for every quote older than a week, then read each one and send it yourself.

3. Summarise a long email thread

Paste in a supplier or customer thread (again, without personal details) and ask for the decision needed, the date it is needed by and anything you have promised. It takes a minute and stops you re-reading twelve messages.

4. Write the product or service description once

If your website copy is thin, give the assistant three facts about the job and ask for a 60-word description in plain English. Keep the facts accurate and cut anything that sounds like marketing for the sake of it.

5. Plan next week in five minutes

Paste in your jobs, deadlines and anything that is blocked, and ask for a day-by-day plan with the awkward task first. It is a simple habit, and it is the one most owners keep using.

A prompt you can copy today

Here is one we would hand a client to get job 1 started. Paste it into your AI assistant, then add your own examples underneath.

You are helping me answer customer enquiries for my small UK business. Below are five real enquiries and the replies I sent. Write three reusable reply templates in the same friendly, direct tone: a quote request, an availability check and a polite no for work outside my area. Use British spelling, keep each under 90 words, and leave square brackets where I need to add a name, price or date.

What it could be worth

Here is a worked example, and it is our own illustrative arithmetic, not a survey result. Suppose these five jobs save you 20 minutes a working day. Over about 240 working days that is 4,800 minutes, or 80 hours a year. If your time is worth £30 an hour to the business, that is about £2,400 of time handed back. Your numbers will differ, so swap in your own. The point is that small, repeatable jobs add up faster than one big project you never start.

Keep a human in the loop

One thing to watch: read everything the assistant writes before it goes out, and keep customer names, phone numbers and card details out of what you paste. That is a sensible habit, and it takes seconds.

Where to go from here

Pick one job from the list and run it every day for a fortnight. If it saves time, keep it. If not, swap it for another. The 19% who feel very confident did not start with a grand plan. They started with one small job and stuck at it.
