Claude Sheets can now build a working staff rota, with tabs, formulas and a running total of everyone's hours, from a single message. December is the month that job hurts most: extra opening hours, holiday requests, two bank holidays and somebody always off sick on the 23rd. Here is how we would set up a Christmas rota for a client this morning, with the prompt to copy and the follow-up questions that do the juggling for you.

The time it eats is real. Legion's 2025 State of the UK Hourly Workforce report found that 65% of UK retail managers spend more than three hours a week on scheduling, and 42% still do it on paper or in Excel, with nearly half ringing or texting staff to fill last-minute gaps. It is a vendor survey of retail managers, but anyone who has built a hospitality or salon rota in December will recognise the number. The work is not hard. It is fiddly: checking who asked for which day off, who is near their hours, and whether every shift has enough people on it.

That fiddly checking is exactly what a spreadsheet with an assistant attached is good at.

Step 1: Gather what you already know

Five minutes with a notepad saves twenty minutes of back and forth. Write down:

  • Everyone's name, their contracted hours a week and any days they never work.
  • Holiday and day-off requests for the period.
  • Your opening hours for each day, including late nights and early closes.
  • How many people you need on each shift, and whether any shift needs a keyholder or a trained person.
  • The days you are shut. In England and Wales this year, Christmas Day falls on Friday 25 December and the Boxing Day bank holiday moves to Monday 28 December, because the 26th is a Saturday.

If you already have last year's rota or a staff list in Excel, import it. Claude builds better from your real data than from a blank page.

Step 2: Paste the prompt

There are two versions. The simple one on our social posts will get you a working rota:

Build a Christmas staff rota for my [type of business] from 21 December to 3 January, with tabs for Rota, Hours and Gaps. Here are my staff, their hours, their requests and our opening times: [paste]. Ask me questions before you build it.

The detailed one below is what we would actually give a client. It takes two more minutes to fill in and saves far more than that in corrections, because it tells Claude who it is working for, what the hard rules are, what "good" looks like and exactly how to lay out each tab. Open Claude, start a sheet (Artifacts tab, then the Sheets tile, or just ask in a chat), and paste this with the square brackets filled in:

Role and goal
You are helping me, the owner of [business name], a [type of business, for example independent café with 8 staff], build the staff rota for Monday 21 December to Sunday 3 January. The goal is a rota I can send to the team today that covers every shift, keeps everyone within their contracted hours and shares the unpopular shifts fairly.

Staff (one line each: name, role, contracted hours a week, days they never work, any skills such as keyholder, first aider or trained on the till)
[paste your list]

Requests already agreed (name, dates, whole or part day)
[paste your list]

Opening hours (each day in the period, including late nights and early closes)
[paste your list]
We are closed on: [for example 25 December, 26 December, 1 January].

Cover needed
Shifts are [for example 8:00 to 14:00 and 14:00 to 20:00]. Each shift needs at least [number] people, and our busiest days, [days], need [number]. Every shift must have at least one of: [keyholders or trained staff].

Hard rules, never break these
1. Nobody works on a day they never work or on an agreed request.
2. Nobody goes over their contracted hours unless I have said they want overtime: [names, or "nobody"].
3. Nobody works a close followed by an open the next morning.
4. Under-18s ([names, or "none"]) finish by [time].

Preferences, follow where you can
Spread late, weekend and Christmas Eve and New Year's Eve shifts evenly. Keep each person's days off together where possible. Put [name] and [name] on different shifts.

How to lay out the sheet
Tab 1, Rota: dates across the top, staff down the side, shift times in each cell, closed days greyed out.
Tab 2, Hours: for each person, hours per week and in total, worked out by formula from the Rota tab, next to their contracted hours, with anyone over or more than 4 hours under highlighted.
Tab 3, Gaps: every shift below the cover needed, every shift without a keyholder, and every request you could not meet, each with the reason.
Tab 4, Notes: any assumption you made.

Before you build it
Read everything above and ask me up to five questions about anything missing or contradictory. Do not guess. Once I have answered, build the sheet, then tell me in three lines how you shared out the unpopular shifts and anything you could not solve.

Why it is built like that: the role and goal tell Claude what a good answer is for; the hard rules are separated from the preferences so it knows what it can bend; the layout section means you get the same tabs every time; and the "ask me first" step catches the gap you forgot before it turns into a wrong rota. Save your filled-in version, and next year it is a two-minute job.

Step 3: Let the Hours and Gaps tabs do the checking

This is where the time comes back. Instead of adding up hours with a pen, the Hours tab shows each person's total against their contract, worked out by formula, so it updates when the rota changes. The Gaps tab is your to-do list: short shifts and requests you could not meet. If both tabs are clear, the rota is done. If not, you fix the gaps rather than re-checking the whole grid.

Check the formulas once, though. Click a total and make sure it is adding the right row. It is a beta, and a spreadsheet you have not checked is just a confident guess.

Step 4: Ask, do not rebuild

December rotas never survive the first week. When something changes, you ask in plain English and Claude edits the sheet, marking what it changed:

  • "Sam is off sick on the 23rd. Who can cover without going over their contracted hours?"
  • "Highlight anyone over their contracted hours in red."
  • "Swap Jo and Alex on the 31st and show me what that does to the Hours tab."
  • "We are now opening until 8pm on the 23rd. Add the extra shift and tell me who is short."

Each of those used to be ten minutes of rubbing out, recounting and phoning round. Now it is a sentence and a check.

Step 5: Get it to your team

Sharing needs a little thought. Claude's share link only opens for people signed in to Claude, and view-only links do not suit a team that does not use it. The simple route is to download the rota as .xlsx or CSV and send it the way you always do, or use "Send to" to carry on in another app. If you print it, export to Excel first, because PDF export is not there yet.

What it could give back

On our own illustrative numbers: if your rota normally takes three hours a week to build and patch, and one prompt plus a few follow-ups gets it to half an hour, that is about two and a half hours back each week. Across the four busiest weeks of December that is around ten hours, in the month you have the least of them. Time yours this week and see.

The bigger win is fewer mistakes. A rota where the totals are worked out by formula, and the gaps are listed for you, is less likely to leave you one person short on Christmas Eve.

One thing to watch

Claude builds the rota, you sign it off. Check every shift against the requests you agreed in person, and keep an eye on young staff and anyone with set hours in their contract, because the assistant only knows what you told it.
