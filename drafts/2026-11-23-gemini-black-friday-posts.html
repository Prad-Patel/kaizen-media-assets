Gemini can now plan and illustrate a week of social posts in your own brand style in one sitting. With Black Friday landing on Friday 27 November, that is the week most owners have the least time to sit down and write. Here is how we would set it up for a client this morning, step by step, with the prompts to copy.

Making posts is now a weekly job for most small firms. A Markel Direct survey of 501 UK small business owners and tradespeople found that 76% spend time every week creating or editing social media content, and 54% spend one to three hours on it. That time usually comes in scraps: a post written between customers, a picture hunted down at 10pm, a caption typed on a phone. Batching it into one sitting, with a tool that already knows what your brand looks like, is the quickest win we know of for a busy week.

What Google added to Gemini

On 20 October Google announced new Gemini tools for small business owners: a business page inside the Gemini app that brings brand building, financial analysis and customer insights together, marketing and finance skills, and new connected apps. Google says it is rolling out globally to all Gemini app users, gradually, so it may not show in every account yet.

Two parts matter for this job:

  • A saved brand style. You give Gemini your logo, colours, fonts or brand sheet once, and it keeps that style for later image requests, so you are not re-explaining your look every time.
  • Connected apps. Google lists its own Business Profile, Google Ads and Workspace alongside third-party apps including Canva, Shopify, Wix, Xero and Zoho. Canva's own connection, launched in May, links to your Canva Brand Kit so designs stay editable.

A note on accuracy: the menu names below come from Google's announcement and early guides, and the layout is still rolling out, so the labels in your app may differ slightly. The prompts work in any Gemini chat whether or not the business page has reached you.

Step 1: Open the business page

Update the Gemini app (or open gemini.google.com) and look in the menu for the new business page. If it is not there yet, do not wait for it. Start a normal chat and carry on with step 3, uploading your logo and colours into the chat instead.

Step 2: Save your brand style once

Upload your logo, your brand colours (the hex codes if you have them) and your fonts, or a clean one-page brand sheet. You can point Gemini at your website instead, but we would not rely on that alone: a website often carries old banners, seasonal colours and stock photos that the tool may pick up as "your style". Name the style after your business so it is easy to call up later.

No brand sheet? Ask Gemini to make one first: "Here is our logo and our website. Pull out our main colours, the feel of our photos and the tone of our writing, and write it up as a one-page brand sheet I can check." Check it, correct it, then save it as your style.

Step 3: Plan the whole week in one prompt

This is the prompt that does most of the work. Fill in the square brackets and paste it:

Plan 5 social posts for [business name], a [what you do] in [town], for Black Friday week, Monday to Friday. Our offer: [offer, price, start and end dates, what is excluded]. For each post give me: a hook line, a caption under 60 words, and one image idea in our saved brand style. Mix it up: one behind the scenes, one product or service close-up, one customer question answered, one reminder, one last-chance post on Friday. No prices, discounts or dates I have not given you. If you are unsure of anything, ask me follow-up questions before you start.

That last line matters more than it looks. It makes Gemini ask about the details it would otherwise guess, such as whether the offer runs through Cyber Monday or whether delivery is included. Edit the plan before you make a single image. It is much quicker to change a sentence than to regenerate five pictures.

Step 4: Make the images

Work through the plan one post at a time:

Make the image for post 1 in our saved brand style, square, with clear space at the top for text. No words, prices or logos in the picture itself.

Then post 2, and so on. We ask for no words in the picture on purpose. AI image models still mangle small lettering, and a misspelt price is the last thing you want on Black Friday. Add your price, dates and logo afterwards as real text, in Canva or whatever you normally use. If you use Canva, the connected app is the shortest route: start a prompt with @Canva and the design opens with your Brand Kit applied, ready to edit.

Step 5: Schedule the lot

Load all five posts into your scheduler in one go. For Facebook and Instagram, Meta Business Suite schedules posts for free. Then add the same offer to your Google Business Profile as an offer post, with the start and end dates, so it shows when people search for you. The point of the sitting is that nothing is left to write on the busiest days of the week.

What it could give back

Take a fairly typical week from the Markel figures: two hours of posting, spread across five days. If one 30 minute sitting covers the same five posts, that is about an hour and a half back each week, or roughly 70 hours over a year of 48 working weeks. That is our own illustrative arithmetic, not a measured result, so time your own first sitting and see. The bigger gain is harder to put a number on: five posts that look like they came from the same business, rather than five that look like they came from five different evenings.

One thing to watch

Read every caption and check every price against your till before anything goes out. AI tools can invent a detail that sounds right, such as free delivery you do not offer, and images can garble logos. On plans, Google says the new tools reach all Gemini app users, but early guides note that the strongest image model sits on paid Google AI plans and free accounts may get a different one, so results can vary. Gemini drafts, you decide.
