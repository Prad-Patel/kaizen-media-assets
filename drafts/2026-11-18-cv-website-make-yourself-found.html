141 applications for every graduate job. That is the latest figure from the Institute of Student Employers, and the wider job market feels just as crowded. When a recruiter has a pile that size, a CV on its own is easy to skim past. A personal CV website is not. It shows your work, your results and your personality in one link, and it now takes an afternoon and costs nothing to build. Here is exactly how we would do it, with the prompt to copy.

Why a CV website puts you ahead

Competition is the obvious reason. Times Higher Education reports that the ISE's 2025-26 survey found 141 applications per graduate vacancy, rising to 172 in London. Experienced hires face their own version of the same pile.

The second reason is newer. Recruiters no longer trust a CV the way they used to. In TestGorilla's 2026 survey of 483 UK talent professionals, three in four said they believe AI writes at least half of the CVs and profiles they see. When every CV reads as polished, proof is what stands out: the campaign you ran, the numbers it hit, the project you can actually show. A website is the natural home for that proof.

It also works while you sleep. Put the link at the top of your CV, in your LinkedIn profile and in your email signature, and anyone curious about you lands on a page you control, rather than whatever a search turns up.

What it looks like

To show what is possible, we built an example site for a made-up marketing coordinator called Jordan Ashby. It has a one-line headline ("I turn small budgets into big results"), three results with numbers, three pieces of selected work, a short experience timeline, skills, and two buttons: download my CV, and book a call. It works on a phone as well as a laptop, and the download button opens a matching one-page CV in the same style, with the same three results at the top. Jordan is fictional, the photo is AI-generated, and every company on the page is made up, but the structure is exactly what we would recommend for a real job hunt.

Step 1: Gather what you already have

You need three things before you start. Your current CV. Three results with a number attached, such as "cut invoice errors by 20%" or "grew followers from 2,000 to 12,000". And one sentence on the job you want and where. The numbers matter most. They are what turn a page about you into a page that proves something.

Step 2: Let the AI interview you, then build it

Most people paste in their CV and ask for a website. You get a prettier copy of the same CV. The better way is to make the AI work like a good recruiter first: read what you have, ask you hard questions, pull out the results you forgot to mention, and only then build. Open Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini (the free plans can all do this), attach or paste your CV, and copy in the prompt below. Fill in the brackets as best you can. Anything you leave blank, it will ask you about.

Who you are working with
You are an experienced UK recruiter and CV writer, and a web designer. Your job is to help me build a one-page personal CV website, and a matching one-page CV, that gets me interviews.

What I am giving you
1. My existing CV: [attach or paste it].
2. My work experience in my own words, including anything my CV leaves out: [list each role, what you did day to day, and anything you are proud of].
3. The job I want: [job title, level, sector, and town or remote]. If I have a job advert in mind, here it is: [paste it].
4. What I want to add: [new skills, courses, projects, volunteering, side work, tools you have learnt].
5. What I want to take away or play down: [old jobs, gaps you would rather explain briefly, anything no longer relevant].

Step one: interview me before you write anything
Ask me questions one at a time, and wait for my answer before asking the next. Ask between eight and twelve questions in total. Grill me on:
- my results: push me for numbers (money saved, sales, time, customers, followers, percentages) and ask how I know them;
- my skills: for each skill I claim, ask for one real example of using it at work;
- the gaps: anything on my CV that a recruiter would question, including dates;
- what makes me different from other people applying for the same job;
- the tone I want: for example warm and friendly, or sharp and professional.
If an answer is vague, ask a follow-up until it is specific. Do not invent anything. If I cannot back a claim up, leave it out.

Step two: show me the plan
Before building, give me: a one-line headline that says what I do best, my three strongest results with numbers, the experience and skills you will include, and what you have cut and why. Wait for me to approve or change it.

Step three: build the website
Write the whole site as a single index.html file with all the styling inside it. Include: the headline, a short intro, my three results as large numbers, two or three examples of my work with the result of each, my experience, my skills, and buttons to download my CV and email me. Make it modern, clean and quick to load, with one accent colour, and make sure it looks good on a phone. Use British English. Do not include my home address, date of birth or personal phone number.

Step four: build the matching CV
Then turn the same content into a one-page A4 CV in the same style, as a separate HTML page I can print to PDF. Put my three results near the top.

Expect the questions to take ten to fifteen minutes. That is the valuable part. The interview is what turns "responsible for social media" into "grew Instagram from 2,000 to 12,000 followers in a year", and that line is what a recruiter remembers.

When you get the files, open the site in your browser. For the CV, open it, choose Print, then Save as PDF, and keep it in the same folder as your index.html so the download button works.

Step 3: Save it, open it, tweak it

Make a new folder on your computer. Copy the website code the AI gives you into a plain text file, save it as index.html in that folder, and put the PDF of your CV next to it. Double-click the file and it opens in your browser, exactly as the world will see it.

Now change anything you do not like by asking in plain words: "make the headline bigger", "use green instead of blue", "add a section for my volunteering". Paste the new code over the old file and refresh. Check it on your phone too, by emailing yourself the file or waiting until the next step.

Step 4: Put it online for free

The easiest route we know is Netlify Drop. Go to Netlify's Drop page, drag your folder onto it, and you get a live link in seconds. Netlify says you do not need an account to start, then you claim the site with a free account so it stays up. Small personal sites like this sit comfortably within the free plan.

If you would rather use something else, GitHub Pages and Cloudflare Pages also host simple sites for free. Each takes a few more clicks, but the result is the same: a public link to your page.

Step 5: Give it a proper address

A free link works, but yourname.co.uk looks sharper on a CV. You can buy a domain from any UK registrar for a small yearly fee and connect it to your site in the host's domain settings. Check the renewal price, not just the first-year offer. This step is optional, so do it once the site is live and you are happy with it.

Step 6: Put the link everywhere

A website nobody visits does nothing. Add the link to the top of your CV next to your email, to your LinkedIn profile (the contact info and Featured sections both take links), to your email signature, and to every application form that has a website or portfolio box. When you message a recruiter, a single line works well: "Here is a one-page overview of my work."

Quick hack: is the job actually right for you?

Before you apply, use the same AI to check the fit. Paste the job description and your new CV into Claude or ChatGPT, with this prompt:

Here is a job description and my CV. Act as the hiring manager for this role. First, list the five things this role needs most and score my CV against each one out of five, with one line on why. Then interview me for the job: ask me the six questions you would most likely ask in a real interview, one at a time, and wait for each answer. After each answer, tell me honestly how strong it was and how to make it stronger. At the end, tell me whether this role is a good fit for me, what I should highlight in my application, and what I should brush up on before an interview.

You get three things from ten minutes of work: an honest read on whether the job suits you, practice answers before the real interview, and a list of what to put at the top of your application. If the scores are low across the board, that is useful too. It saves you an application that was never going to land.

What it could do for you

A CV tells a recruiter what you say you did. A site shows them. In a pile of 141 applications, being the candidate with a clean link, real results and a "book a call" button is a simple way to be remembered. It also gives you something to send after an interview, and a place to keep adding work as you go.

The cost is an afternoon. With the prompt above, a first version can be live within the hour, then spend the rest of the time making the words better.

One thing to watch

Keep it truthful and keep it private. Recruiters are checking claims more carefully than ever, so only include results you can talk through in an interview. Leave your home address and personal phone number off the page, and use an email address you are happy to have public. Read every line the AI writes before you publish. It can make things sound grander than they were, and on a CV site, accuracy is the whole point.
