Our view: this is the week to make sure that when a customer asks ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude where to buy, the answer can include you, with the right price. Black Friday is on 27 November, so there are five days left. The five checks below take an afternoon, and none of them needs a developer.

We use these AI tools every day to run Kaizen, and we test how they describe businesses before we build anything for a client. The pattern is simple. An AI assistant can only recommend what it can read and trust. If your offer lives in a banner image, or your opening hours are out of date, the assistant either skips you or gets you wrong.

Why this matters this week

The shift is fast. YouGov found planned AI use among likely UK Black Friday and Cyber Monday shoppers almost doubled, from 19% to 34%. Britain is still behind other countries on AI shopping (YouGov ranks it 18th of 19 markets), which means the habit is still forming here, and the businesses that show up well now are the ones shoppers will see first.

Globally it is further along. DHL's Black Friday research, published on 19 November and based on its 2026 survey of shoppers in 29 countries, found that 88% of Black Friday shoppers have used AI to compare prices or plan to. Half of those AI-assisted shoppers went on to buy during Black Friday.

Not every survey agrees on the size of it. KPMG UK's survey of 3,000 consumers put the share who will use AI chatbots to search for Black Friday offers at 12%, roughly flat on last year. The gap comes from different questions and samples. Even at the low end, that is more than one shopper in ten asking a chatbot before they buy, and the higher figures come from people who already plan to shop the sales, which is exactly the group you want.

Check 1: put your offer in plain text, on one page

Most small shops announce Black Friday with a graphic: a big banner, a percentage, a countdown. People can read that. AI assistants are much better at reading plain text on the page than text baked into an image, so a banner on its own is easy to miss.

Give the offer its own short section, or its own page, written as plain sentences:

  • what is on offer (the product or service, by its proper name)
  • the sale price and the normal price
  • start and end dates, written out ("Friday 27 November to Monday 30 November")
  • what is included, and anything excluded

If you sell through Shopify, Etsy, Squarespace or WooCommerce, put the same details in the product description itself, not only in a site-wide banner. Keep the wording identical everywhere so nothing contradicts itself.

Check 2: make the was-price honest

Shoppers are sceptical of Black Friday, and AI tools are being used precisely to check whether a deal is real. Which? research has repeatedly found that most Black Friday "deals" it tracked were the same price or cheaper at other times of the year, and KPMG reports that among people not planning to buy, half say they do not believe the discounts are better than any other time.

The upside for a small business is that honesty is easy to show. Use a normal price you have actually charged recently, say so ("our usual price since September"), and if the deal is a bundle or a free extra rather than a cut, say that plainly. A clear, believable offer is the kind an assistant can repeat with confidence, and the kind a customer trusts when they click through.

Check 3: update your Google Business Profile today

When someone asks an assistant for a local shop, it leans on public listings as well as your website. Your Google Business Profile is the one most small businesses already have, and it is quick to fix:

  • Add an Offer post with the deal, the dates and a link to the offer page.
  • Set your special hours for the sale weekend, so nobody turns up to a closed door.
  • Check your categories, products and description still match what you sell this season.

It takes about fifteen minutes, and it helps ordinary Google search and Maps as well, so it pays off whichever way a customer looks.

Check 4: answer the questions shoppers actually ask

An AI assistant is often asked a follow-up, not just "where can I buy this?" but "will it arrive before Christmas?" or "can I return it?" If your site answers those clearly, the assistant can too. If it does not, a competitor that does may get the recommendation.

Add a short question-and-answer block to the offer page covering:

  • last order date for Christmas delivery, and delivery cost
  • returns: how long, and who pays
  • stock: limited numbers, or while stocks last
  • click and collect, if you offer it

Write each answer as a full sentence. "Order by 18 December for UK delivery before Christmas" is far more useful to a reader, human or AI, than "see delivery page".

Check 5: ask the AI yourself, then fix what it gets wrong

This is the one most owners never do, and it is the most revealing. Open ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude and ask each one the question your customer would ask. Here is a prompt you can paste:

I am looking for a Black Friday deal on [your product or service] from a small independent business in or near [your town]. Which businesses would you suggest, what are their current offers, and how did you find them?

Then ask directly about you:

What do you know about [your business name] in [your town]? What do they sell, what are their opening hours, and do they have a Black Friday offer?

Note anything missing or wrong: an old price, the wrong hours, a product you no longer sell. Almost every error traces back to something out of date on your website or your listings, so fix it at the source. Results vary by tool and by day, and assistants that do not search the web will lag behind, so treat this as a spot check rather than a guarantee. Run it again after your changes.

What it could do for you

The payoff is being the business the assistant names, with the right deal and the right hours, at the moment a customer is ready to buy. On YouGov's figures, roughly one in three of the people planning to shop the sales may now ask an AI first. You do not need a new website or a new tool for that. You need an offer written so a machine can read it, a listing that is up to date, and ten minutes asking the assistants what they think of you.
