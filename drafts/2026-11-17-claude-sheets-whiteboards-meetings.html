Claude now builds spreadsheets, runs a shared whiteboard and, if your team is on the right plan, joins your Google Meet call and writes the action items before anyone hangs up. Anthropic launched Claude Sheets and Claude Whiteboards in beta on 10 November, and Claude Meetings followed in beta on 12 November. Together they are the closest thing yet to a Claude version of the office suite.

We use Claude every day to run Kaizen, so we read both announcements line by line. Here is what each tool does, what it could take off a small business owner's plate in a normal week, and the catches worth knowing before you switch anything off.

What Anthropic actually launched

There are four products in this release, and the names matter because the coverage has been muddled:

  • Claude Sheets (beta): Claude builds and edits spreadsheets with tabs, formulas, formatting and drop-downs. Every edit Claude makes is outlined so you can see what changed, and you can share a sheet to work on it together.
  • Claude Whiteboards (beta): a shared board where you and your team brainstorm with Claude. It can turn a spoken plan into a board with a timeline, owners and dates, group notes by theme and draft the action items.
  • Claude Data (now generally available): the product that was called Claude Dashboards. It needs a data connection such as a CRM or a data warehouse, so it is aimed at larger teams.
  • Claude Meetings (beta): Claude joins a Google Meet call as a visible participant, answers questions in the chat or out loud, writes notes and action items, and can build a document or whiteboard with you during the call.

According to Anthropic's launch article, Sheets, Whiteboards and Data are on all paid plans and work on the web, desktop and mobile apps. On Team plans all three are switched on by default. On Enterprise, Sheets and Whiteboards start switched off until an admin turns them on.

Claude Sheets: the spreadsheet you describe instead of build

Most small businesses run on a handful of spreadsheets that someone built years ago and nobody quite trusts: the quote tracker, the stock list, the cash flow forecast with a formula that broke in March. Sheets lets you describe the spreadsheet you want in plain English and have Claude build it with the tabs and formulas already in place.

Where it could help in a normal week:

  • A quote tracker with a tab per month, a status drop-down and a total of what is still outstanding.
  • A simple cash flow view from a list of invoices and bills you paste in.
  • Tidying a messy export from your booking or accounts system into something you can read.

The honest limit: Anthropic describes Sheets as having "simple spreadsheet functionality" for now. For anything heavy, you export it to Excel (.xlsx) or CSV, or use Claude for Excel inside Microsoft 365. So this is a fast way to start or fix a sheet, not a replacement for Excel or Google Sheets if your business lives in complex models.

Claude Whiteboards: from messy plan to owners and dates

Whiteboards is the one we expect most owners to underrate. Plenty of small teams plan on a real whiteboard, a photo of it, or a WhatsApp thread, and the plan quietly dies because nobody wrote down who was doing what by when.

Whiteboards does that last step for you. Talk through a plan, a product launch, a Christmas rota or a website rebuild, and Claude lays it out as a board with a timeline, owners and dates, then drafts the action items. You can select part of the board and ask Claude about it, turn the board into a document or a slide deck, or move it to Miro or Lucid if that is where your team already works.

If you pay for a whiteboard tool mainly for a monthly planning session, this is worth a test before your next renewal.

Claude Meetings: notes and action items before you hang up

This is the headline feature, and it lands on a real problem. In Owl Labs' 2026 State of Hybrid Work survey of 2,014 UK employees, 41 per cent said they experience meeting fatigue, and the most common reason given was meetings that run too long (54 per cent). A tool that ends the meeting with the notes already written does not shorten the meeting, but it does remove the half hour of tidying up afterwards.

Here is what Anthropic's Claude Meetings announcement says it does:

  • Claude joins your Google Meet call when you add it, or automatically from your calendar if you switch that on.
  • It writes notes and action items, and anyone can ask it for a recap before the call ends.
  • It can answer questions during the call and build a document or whiteboard with you while you talk.
  • Afterwards, each meeting gets its own page with the notes, transcript and anything Claude built. It keeps the transcript and summary, not the audio.

To put a rough number on it: if you spend 15 minutes writing up notes after each of four meetings a week, that is an hour a week, or about 48 hours over a 48-week year. That is our own illustrative arithmetic, not a figure from Anthropic, and your own meeting load will differ.

The catches worth knowing

These are new tools, and the small print matters:

  • Everything is beta except Claude Data. Expect rough edges and changes.
  • Paid plans only. Sheets and Whiteboards need a paid plan. Claude Meetings is narrower: Team and Enterprise plans only, so a single Pro subscription will not get it.
  • Google Meet only, for now. Anthropic says other video platforms are "coming soon" but has not named them. If your clients book you on Zoom or Microsoft Teams, Meetings cannot help yet.
  • Outside guests switch it off by default. Claude stays out of calls with people from outside your organisation, and leaves if one joins, unless an admin changes that. Great for internal stand-ups, less useful for client calls until you change the setting.
  • Tell people they are being transcribed. Anthropic itself advises confirming everyone is comfortable before adding Claude to a call. In the UK that is also good practice under data protection rules.
  • Pricing after the beta is not set. Notes and transcripts are free on Team and Enterprise during the beta, and documents and whiteboards made in meetings are free until 10 February 2027. Anthropic has not said what happens after that.

On UK availability: neither announcement names any country limits, and Claude's paid plans are sold in the UK. Meetings is "starting to roll out", so it may take a few days to reach your account. Plan prices are set in US dollars on Claude's pricing page, so check the figure shown in your account before budgeting, and allow for VAT.

What we would tell a client to do this week

Do not cancel anything yet. Pick one job for each tool and see whether it saves you time:

  1. Sheets: ask Claude to rebuild the one spreadsheet you dread opening. Try: "Build me a quote tracker with a tab for each month, a status drop-down (sent, chasing, won, lost) and a total of what is still outstanding."
  2. Whiteboards: run your next planning chat through it and see if the action items come out with owners and dates you would actually use.
  3. Meetings: if you are on a Team plan and use Google Meet, add Claude to one internal meeting and compare its notes with your own.

If two out of three save you time, that is a good sign the office apps you pay for today deserve a second look at renewal. If they do not, you have lost an afternoon, not a subscription.
