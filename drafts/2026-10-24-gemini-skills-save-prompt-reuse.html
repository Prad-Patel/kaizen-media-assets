Google has started rolling out Skills in the Gemini app and across Google Workspace, and for a small business it is one of the more useful launches this autumn. A Skill is a saved set of instructions that Gemini can use whenever you ask. You write down how you want a job done once, give it a name, and from then on you type a slash and that name instead of re-explaining yourself. If you keep pasting the same brief into Gemini every week, this is the feature to try first.

What Gemini Skills are, in plain English

According to Google's Workspace Updates announcement, Skills are reusable instructions that guide Gemini to follow your rules, templates and reference files. You can build one from scratch, create one from a chat that went well, or ask Gemini to help turn your past chats into one. To use it, you type a slash followed by the Skill's name. Gemini can also apply a relevant Skill by itself when your new prompt matches it, and you can stack more than one Skill in the same conversation.

Skills can draw on files as well as instructions. Think of a price list, a tone-of-voice sheet or a standard terms document sitting behind the Skill, so every answer starts from your material rather than a blank page. Google's own examples include a brand voice Skill for drafting posts, which is exactly the sort of job a small business repeats every week.

Skills are replacing Gems, Google's earlier way of saving a custom Gemini. Google's help page on the move from Gems to skills says existing Gems will be recreated as Skills when they are retired, with timing that depends on your account type. If you already have a Gem you rely on, it is not wasted effort, but it is worth knowing it is on the way out.

Why a saved Skill is worth setting up

Here is the arithmetic, and it is an illustration rather than a promise. Say you spend ten minutes each time you brief an AI properly: who the customer is, how you write, what to leave out, what the finished thing should look like. If you do that five times a week, that is 50 minutes a week, or about 43 hours across a year (10 minutes x 5 x 52 = 2,600 minutes). A saved Skill does not remove the work of the job itself. It removes the re-briefing, and it makes the briefing the same every time, which is often where the real gain is. The tenth quote reply sounds like the first one.

Your own numbers will differ, so swap in your own. The pattern holds for any task you do more than once a week and explain the same way each time.

Five Skills a small business could set up this week

These are the jobs we would start with, because each one is repeated, has a clear right answer, and is easy to check at a glance.

  1. Quote and enquiry replies. Your standard opening, what you include in a quote, your payment terms and how you sign off. Paste in the enquiry, get a draft in your voice.
  2. Social post drafts. Your tone, words you never use, the length that suits each platform, and the call to action you always end on.
  3. Product or service descriptions. Your format, the details every listing needs, and a couple of examples of ones you are proud of.
  4. Customer follow-ups. A polite chase after a quote, a thank-you after a job, a request for a review. Same structure, different names.
  5. Weekly summary. Paste in the week's notes, get a short list of what is done, what is waiting and what needs a decision.

How to build your first one in about 15 minutes

Start with the job you repeat most. Do not try to build all five at once.

  1. Do the job well once in Gemini. Keep going until you are happy with the result.
  2. Turn that chat into a Skill. Google says you can create a Skill from a chat. Ask Gemini to write the instructions that would reproduce this result next time.
  3. Read the instructions and tighten them. Cut anything vague. Add the rules you actually care about, such as British spelling, no jargon or a maximum length.
  4. Add your reference material. A price list, a template, two or three good past examples.
  5. Test it on a real task. Type a slash and the Skill's name, paste in a genuine enquiry, and compare the draft with what you would have written.
  6. Adjust once, then use it for a week. Fix whatever annoyed you and leave it alone until the week is out.

Here is a starting prompt you can paste into Gemini to get the first draft of a Skill:

"Look at the conversation above. Write a reusable set of instructions that would make you produce the same quality of result next time. Include my tone of voice, what to always include, what to never include, and the format of the finished piece. Keep it under 200 words and use plain English."

What to check before you rely on it

Skills are new, and the rollout is staged. Google's announcement says they are arriving over the coming weeks, and some third-party coverage gives specific dates that Google itself does not. If you cannot see Skills in your account, a Workspace admin may need to switch on Gemini features in the admin console, so it is worth asking whoever manages your Google account.

One detail to know from Google's help page: Skills in the Gemini app and in Workspace apps are not automatically synced. If you want the same Skill in both places, you may need to recreate it where you want to use it. It is a small job, but it is better to find out on day one than on day thirty.

As with any AI draft, read what comes out before it goes to a customer. The Skill makes the draft consistent. It does not make it correct, and the quality still depends on the instructions and files you give it.

Where this fits with the other AI tools

Gemini is not the only place this idea has landed. Claude has Projects, which hold standing instructions and files for a piece of work, and ChatGPT has its own version of saved instructions. They differ in the detail, but the principle is shared: write the brief once, keep it somewhere, and stop typing it out. We would not pick a tool for this feature alone. If you already live in Google Workspace, with Gmail, Docs and Drive, Skills are the natural place to start because they sit where your work already is.

What we would do first

If we were sitting next to you, we would pick the one message you write most often, get Gemini to produce a version you are happy with, and save it as a Skill before lunch. Then we would use it for a week and see how many minutes it gives back. That small test costs you an afternoon and tells you whether it is worth doing for the other four jobs.
