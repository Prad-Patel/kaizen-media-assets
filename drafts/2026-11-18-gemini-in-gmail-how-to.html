Microsoft's own data says the average worker now receives about 117 emails a day, and skims most of them in under a minute. If you run a small business on Google Workspace, Gemini in Gmail can take two of the slowest jobs off that pile: reading the long back-and-forth threads, and writing the replies you keep putting off. Google started switching both on for Workspace accounts on 10 November. Here is exactly how we would set it up for a client, with the prompts to copy.

What changed this month

Gemini in Gmail is not new, but until recently the best of it sat behind the more expensive plans and add-ons. From 10 November 2026, Google began rolling out two features, AI Overview summaries of long threads and "Help me write" for drafting, to Workspace users aged 18 and over. TalkAndroid reports the rollout can take up to 15 days, so some accounts will not see it until around 25 November. TechBriefly notes that Gmail's smart features have to be switched on for either to appear.

Two honest caveats before you start. First, if you use a free personal Gmail address rather than a Workspace account, Google has so far launched the free versions of these tools in the US first, so a UK personal account may not show them yet. Second, the features are on by default, but an administrator can switch them off in the Admin console. If you pay someone to look after your Google account, check with them first.

Why this is worth ten minutes of setup

The figure at the top comes from Microsoft's June 2025 Work Trend Index report, "Breaking down the infinite workday", which is based on anonymised Microsoft 365 activity rather than a survey. The same report found that 40% of people online at 6am are already going through email. Small business owners will recognise the pattern: the inbox is the first job of the day and the last one at night.

Most of that email does not need deep thought. It needs reading quickly and answering clearly. That is exactly what these two tools do, and the reason we like them for owners is that they live inside Gmail. No new app, no copying and pasting between tabs.

Step 1: Check smart features are switched on

On a computer, open Gmail, select the gear icon, then See all settings. On the General tab, find the smart features section and make sure smart features are switched on, then save at the bottom of the page. Google moves these menus around from time to time, so if the wording is slightly different on your screen, look for "smart features" rather than an exact label.

If you still see nothing after 25 November, ask whoever manages your Workspace account to check that Gemini in Gmail is enabled for your organisation.

Step 2: Let Gemini read the long thread for you

Open any email thread with several replies in it. With the rollout complete, Gemini adds an AI Overview at the top that pulls out the key points: who asked for what, what was agreed, and what is still open. On the Gmail app, look for the summary option at the top of the conversation.

This is the bit that saves the most time on supplier chains, quote negotiations and anything that has bounced between three people. Read the overview, then scroll to the one message that matters, rather than reading all twelve.

Step 3: Draft the reply with Help me write

Select Reply, then select Help me write (the pencil icon with a sparkle). Type what you want to say in plain words, and Gemini drafts the full email. The trick is to give it the facts it cannot guess. Copy this and fill in the brackets:

Reply to [name]. Confirm we can do [the job] on [date] for [price including VAT]. Ask them to confirm by [day]. Friendly and plain, under 80 words, sign off as [your name].

Select Create, read the draft, and either insert it or ask for another go. The draft lands in your reply box, so nothing sends until you press Send.

Step 4: Turn rough notes into a proper email

This is our favourite and the least known. Type your reply as rough notes, the way you would text a colleague: "can do tues not weds, price same, need deposit first". Once a draft has around a dozen words, Gmail offers to refine it. Choose Polish and Gemini turns your notes into a tidy email in your words. The other options are Formalise, Elaborate and Shorten, which do what they say.

Polish is the one to use when you know exactly what you want to say and only need it to read well. It keeps your facts and fixes the tone.

Step 5: Three prompts for the emails owners put off

Save these somewhere handy and paste them into Help me write when the email turns up:

  • Chasing a quote: "Write a friendly follow-up to [name] about the quote we sent on [date] for [job]. Ask if they have any questions and whether they would like to book in. No pressure, under 60 words."
  • Saying no politely: "Politely decline [name]'s request for [job]. We are not the right fit because [reason]. Suggest they try [alternative] and thank them for thinking of us. Under 70 words."
  • Moving a booking: "Tell [name] we need to move their appointment from [date] to [new date] because [reason]. Apologise once, offer [option], and ask them to reply to confirm. Under 70 words."

Each one ends with a word limit on purpose. Short emails get answered, and a limit stops Gemini padding the draft with phrases you would never use.

What it could give back

Here is a simple illustration, on our own assumptions rather than a study. Say the summaries and drafts save you 15 minutes a day, a couple of long threads you no longer read end to end and a few replies you no longer write from scratch. Across five days a week and 48 working weeks, that is 60 hours a year, about a working week and a half. Time yourself for a week before and after and you will know your own number.

The bigger win is the emails that stop sitting in your inbox for three days because they felt like hard work. A quote chased on time is worth more than the minutes it saves.

One thing to watch

Read every draft before you send it. Gemini works from what is in the email, so it can miss context that only you know, or sound more certain about a date or price than you are. A summary is a shortcut to the right message, not a replacement for reading the one that matters. Gemini drafts, you decide.

If you are not on Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 Copilot and ChatGPT can do similar jobs, but you will be copying text between apps. For owners already on Gmail, this is the lowest-effort AI upgrade available this month.
